TOMATO EARLY BLIGHT

Symptoms:
Tomato early blight commonly produces dark brown to black spots on older leaves.
The spots may develop concentric ring patterns and can cause surrounding leaf tissue
to turn yellow. As the disease progresses, leaves may fall from the plant.

General management:
- Remove and dispose of severely affected leaves.
- Avoid unnecessary wetting of leaves during irrigation.
- Provide adequate spacing between plants to improve air circulation.
- Remove plant debris from around affected plants.
- Monitor plants regularly for new symptoms.
- Use locally approved disease-management products according to their label and
  agricultural extension recommendations.

Prevention:
- Maintain good field sanitation.
- Avoid prolonged leaf wetness where practical.
- Rotate crops where appropriate.
- Use healthy planting material.
- Monitor plants regularly, especially during favorable disease conditions.


TOMATO LATE BLIGHT

Symptoms:
Late blight can produce dark, water-soaked-looking lesions on leaves and stems.
Under favorable conditions the disease can spread rapidly.

General management:
- Remove heavily affected plant material where appropriate.
- Improve air circulation around plants.
- Avoid prolonged leaf wetness.
- Monitor plants frequently.
- Follow local agricultural extension recommendations for disease management.

Prevention:
- Use healthy planting material.
- Maintain good sanitation.
- Monitor weather and crop conditions.
- Avoid unnecessary overhead irrigation.


APPLE SCAB

Symptoms:
Apple scab can cause olive-green to brown lesions on leaves and fruit.
Severe infections may cause premature leaf drop and affect fruit quality.

General management:
- Remove fallen infected leaves and plant debris where practical.
- Maintain good orchard sanitation.
- Monitor trees regularly.
- Follow locally approved disease-management recommendations.

Prevention:
- Maintain orchard hygiene.
- Monitor trees during periods favorable to disease development.
- Use resistant varieties when appropriate.
